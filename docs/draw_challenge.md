# CHALLENGE 5: WATER SECURITY

_Reliable access. Resilient communities. A safer future._

---

## 1. Overview & Context Areas

- **Climate and Environmental Risks:** Droughts, floods, typhoons, changing rainfall patterns
- **Infrastructure and Service Coverage:** Pipelines, treatment, distribution, and reliability
- **Water Availability:** Rivers, springs, groundwater, reservoirs, and other sources
- **Community Access:** Sufficient, safe, and affordable water for all households
- **Livelihoods and Food Security:** Fisheries, aquaculture, agriculture, and local economy
- **Disasters and Emergencies:** Service disruptions, contamination, infrastructure damage

---

## 2. What is the Problem?

Water security means more than having a water source nearby[cite: 1]. A community can have a river, spring, groundwater source, reservoir, or water system and still experience water insecurity if people cannot reliably access sufficient, safe, and affordable water when they need it[cite: 1].

---

## 3. Why Does it Matter?

Reliable access to water is essential for public health, livelihoods, food security, local economic activity, and community resilience[cite: 1]. When water is not available, not safe, or not affordable, it can affect households, agriculture, fisheries, businesses, and essential services[cite: 1]. Water security is therefore both a resource issue and an access issue[cite: 1].

---

## 4. The Situation

Across Region VIII, communities differ in their geographic conditions, settlement patterns, infrastructure, and economic circumstances[cite: 1]. Some areas have relatively reliable water services, while others experience limited coverage, intermittent supply, seasonal shortages, or disruptions due to disasters and infrastructure failures[cite: 1]. The situation can be more difficult for geographically isolated, underserved, or economically constrained communities, or those where extending infrastructure is challenging[cite: 1].

---

## 5. The Challenge

> **How can we achieve equitable and reliable access to water?**[cite: 1]  
> The concern is not simply whether sufficient water exists somewhere[cite: 1]. It is whether people can actually access the water they need, when they need it, and under circumstances that are safe, reliable, and reasonably affordable—especially when normal systems are disrupted by drought, disasters, contamination, infrastructure failures, or other emergencies[cite: 1].

---

## 6. What Could a Solution Address?

Solutions may explore, but are not limited to[cite: 1]:

- **Source development and protection**[cite: 1]
- **Alternative and decentralized systems**[cite: 1]
- **Monitoring and early warning systems**[cite: 1]
- **Community-based approaches and inclusive access**[cite: 1]
- **Resilience to disasters and climate risks**[cite: 1]
- **Affordability and sustainable financing**[cite: 1]
