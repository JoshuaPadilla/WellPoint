## I. OVERVIEW

The **rSCENE 2026 Hackathon** is a two-level, 48-hour programming and innovation challenge that brings together developers, designers, innovators, and technology enthusiasts to create practical digital solutions to pressing water-related challenges faced by the different Local Government Units (LGUs) in the region. 

Participating teams will be presented with specific water-related problem areas and will develop a functional prototype addressing it, with **Level-1** judged by technical experts and **Level-2** evaluated by professional end-users. The activity promotes innovation, user-centered design, technical functionality, feasibility, and solutions with potential for adoption and scaling in local communities.

---

## II. OBJECTIVES

1. To address pressing water-related challenges of local government units and communities in Region VIII through innovative and technology-driven solutions.
2. To engage diverse participants in developing practical solutions based on the actual needs and experiences of communities and local governments.
3. To promote innovation, collaboration, and user-centered problem-solving in improving water resource management, water services, environmental sustainability, and water security.
4. To identify and showcase solutions with potential for adoption, implementation, and scaling to benefit communities and strengthen local government capacity.

## V. THEMES AND PROBLEM STATEMENTS

### Field: Sustainable Water Solutions
The following problem areas identify key water-related challenges affecting communities and local government units in Region VIII. These profiles provide regional context and are not intended to prescribe a particular technology, product, or approach. Participants are encouraged to understand the root problem, identify stakeholder needs, and build appropriate, feasible digital solutions.

The 5 key problem areas are:
1. Watershed Preservation & Management
2. Water Economics
3. Water-Related Infrastructure
4. Circular Economy in Water Resources
5. Water Security

---

### a. Watershed Preservation & Management
A watershed is a connected system in which forests, rivers, streams, agricultural lands, settlements, groundwater, and human activities interact. What happens in one part affects water availability and quality elsewhere. Communities depend on these areas for domestic supply, agriculture, fisheries, and livelihoods, while watersheds also provide essential ecological regulation.

- **The Situation:** Many communities in Region VIII, especially upland and urbanizing areas, rely on limited watershed zones that face competing demands from agriculture, settlements, and resource extraction. Information across watersheds is often fragmented among different agencies, offices, and communities.
- **The Challenge:** Maintaining the long-term capacity of limited watershed and water-source areas to support communities and ecosystems while navigating continuous development pressures and fragmented governance data.

---

### b. Water Economics
Water is both a basic human need and an economic resource requiring capital to capture, treat, distribute, maintain, and regulate. Different uses—household, irrigation, aquaculture, fisheries, livestock, tourism, and commercial—have competing values and requirements.

- **The Situation:** Region VIII’s economy heavily depends on agriculture, fisheries, aquaculture, and natural-resource livelihoods. Water availability directly impacts household incomes and local economic stability. LGUs must balance supply costs, user affordability, and limited public budgets.
- **The Challenge:** Balancing water as an affordable, accessible basic necessity with the financial realities of capital investment, infrastructure operations, fair allocation, and local economic development under constrained resources.

---

### c. Water-Related Infrastructure
Reliable water delivery relies on complex, often invisible systems: intake structures, treatment facilities, reservoirs, pump stations, transmission networks, distribution pipelines, flow meters, and drainage facilities.

- **The Situation:** Growing communities require expanded, upgraded services (such as moving toward Level III distribution systems and advanced water treatment). While smart technologies offer optimization opportunities, they also raise concerns regarding operation, maintenance costs, technical capacity, and reliability in local operational conditions.
- **The Challenge:** Ensuring infrastructure delivers safe, reliable, and sustainable service across its entire lifecycle—from source to tap—while addressing aging assets, shifting demand, maintenance requirements, and unforeseen disruptions.

---

### d. Circular Economy in Water Resources
Conventional water systems follow a linear model: *source $\rightarrow$ use $\rightarrow$ discharge $\rightarrow$ treat/dispose*. However, used water retains potential utility depending on quality and treatment options.

- **The Situation:** Significant volumes of domestic and commercial greywater are discarded daily despite potential for cascade uses (e.g., sanitation, cleaning, urban irrigation). Realizing circularity requires addressing water quality, safety, contamination, behavioral habits, and infrastructure feasibility.
- **The Challenge:** Shifting from a single-use mindset toward circular water resource systems by developing practical, safe, cost-effective, and user-friendly recycling/reuse solutions suited to community realities.

---

### e. Water Security
Water security extends beyond mere proximity to a water source; it requires consistent, safe, reliable, and affordable access when needed.

- **The Situation:** Geographic disparities across Region VIII mean isolated, rural, or economically constrained areas face intermittent supply, seasonal shortages, or vulnerability to severe disaster damage (e.g., typhoons, droughts, infrastructure breakdowns).
- **The Challenge:** Bridging the gap between water availability and actual, equitable access, particularly for underserved, geographically isolated, or disaster-vulnerable populations.

## VI. RULES AND REQUIREMENTS

### 1. Project Scope
- Teams must develop a working prototype or proof of concept addressing their assigned challenge within the allotted time.
- Solutions must be software-based: web applications, mobile apps, data platforms, dashboards, analytical models, or digital tools.

### 2. Technical Limitations & Tools
- **Hardware projects are not allowed.** However, teams may use simulated data or pre-existing hardware/sensor telemetry data.
- Any programming languages, frameworks, libraries, or development stacks are permitted.
- All code/work must be original and produced during the event; pre-existing codebases/projects are prohibited.
- Open-source libraries, packages, and public APIs are permitted with proper attribution.

### 3. Team Conduct
- Collaboration is strictly limited to registered team members; external assistance during hacking hours is prohibited.
- Professionalism, ethical behavior, and respect are required throughout the competition.

### 4. Submissions
- **Level 1:** Submit the working prototype (executable link/repository/deployment) and presentation slide deck by **8:00 AM on Day 2**.
- **Level 2:** Submit the refined prototype and updated 5-minute slide deck by **8:00 AM on Day 3**.
- Late submissions will not be accepted except in verified cases of organizer-side technical failure.

## VII. JUDGING CRITERIA

### 1. Level 1: Technical Screening (Technical Judges)
*Evaluates technical merit, development execution, and viability produced during the 24-hour sprint.*
- **Panel:** IT Professionals, Software Engineers, CICTO Staff.

| Criteria | Description | Weight |
| :--- | :--- | :---: |
| **Feasibility & Implementability** | Practicality of deployment in an LGU/community context; required data, infrastructure, operational skills, and realism beyond a prototype stage. | **40%** |
| **Problem Relevance & Potential Impact** | How directly the solution addresses the assigned water challenge and delivers tangible improvements for communities and LGUs. | **25%** |
| **Technical Viability** | Technical soundness, system architecture, data flow, code structure, appropriate technology choice, and working core logic. | **15%** |
| **Innovation & Creativity** | Originality of approach, novel problem-solving methodology, or inventive integration of tools/data. | **10%** |
| **Sustainability & Scalability** | Potential for long-term maintenance, adaptability to other LGUs, modularity, and resource efficiency. | **10%** |
| **TOTAL** | | **100%** |

*Outcome: The Top 4 scoring teams advance to Level 2 (Final Judging).*

---

### 2. Level 2: Final Judging (Professional End-User Judges)
*Evaluates holistic value, user experience, end-user practicality, and final execution.*
- **Panel:** Academic leaders, Water Utility Executives, Engineering & Software Professionals.

| Criteria | Description | Weight |
| :--- | :--- | :---: |
| **Technical Functionality & Feasibility** | System execution, robustness, stability of demo, sound engineering, and practical adoption potential. | **27%** |
| **Innovation & Creativity** | Distinctiveness of the idea and creative application of digital methods to solve the problem. | **17%** |
| **Relevance to Assigned Challenge** | Alignment with real end-user needs and specific pain points of the assigned theme. | **17%** |
| **User Experience (UX) & Design** | Interface intuition, visual accessibility, ease of navigation, and overall user-centric design. | **13%** |
| **Scalability & Sustainability** | Long-term operational viability, expansion capability, maintenance burden, and multi-community applicability. | **13%** |
| **Presentation & Demonstration** | Clarity of pitch, quality of live demo, ability to field questions, and persuasive articulation of value. | **13%** |
| **TOTAL** | | **100%** |
